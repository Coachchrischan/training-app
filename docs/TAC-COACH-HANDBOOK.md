# TAC Coach Handbook

**Teneriffe Athletic Club — 76 Commercial Rd, Teneriffe QLD**

Version 1.0 · 16 September 2026 · Owner: Chris Chan (Coach & Programming Manager)

This is the document a new coach is run through on their first morning. It is also the document a
current coach re-reads when something changes. If a question comes up on the floor that this
document does not answer, that is a gap in the document, not a gap in the coach. Tell Chris and it
gets added.

Fields marked **[CONFIRM]** are waiting on a decision or a detail. They are listed together in
§12 so they are easy to chase.

## 1. The 60-second version

We run three products: **Strength**, **Conditioning** and **HYROX**, plus a **Saturday Community
Day**. Strength and Conditioning are programmed centrally by Chris. HYROX is programmed by Jordan
from 21 September. Coaches deliver the programmed session; they do not rewrite it on the floor.

Every session has the same shape: **brief and warm-up ~10 min → the work → ~5 min cool-down.**
Every target has three lanes: **Base, Build, Push.** Every class has a cap and members must book.

The thing we are selling is not the workout. It is that the workout is clearly explained, correctly
scaled for the person in front of you, and finishes on time. Get those three right and the
programming does the rest.

**What it is.** A six-month plan built from three- to four-week blocks. Compound lifts stay
consistent through a phase; accessories rotate between blocks. Members log to TrainHeroic, which
holds their maxes and prescribes loads off them.

**Upper Body (Tuesday).** Structured pressing, pulling and accessory work. Heavy loading, strict
tempo, clean execution. Bench variations, rows, overhead pressing, isolation for shoulders, arms
and upper back. Push/pull balance is the organising idea.

**Lower Body (Thursday).** Squats, deadlifts, lunges, hinge variations. Lower rep ranges, longer
rests, progressive loading through the posterior chain. Upper moved to Tuesday specifically so run
club members can make Lower on Thursday.

**Full Body (Saturday, 6am).** The third day exists so members hit enough weekly sets to actually
progress, and so someone with a busy week still gets a second session in. It runs in the back
functional area because there is more space for dynamic movements without disturbing the general
gym floor. **[CONFIRM: full class description for GymMaster — Alanah asked for this and it is
still outstanding.]**

**Coaching it.** The session is on TrainHeroic and on the screen. Your job is the brief, the
scaling call, and the eyes. Do not add exercises. If something in the programming does not work for
a person in front of you, scale it and tell Chris afterwards so the program gets fixed for
everybody.

## 4. The Group Fitness Room

Johno's layout as of 14 September 2026. **Gear does not move during a class.** Everything is set
before the door opens.

| Station | Count |
|---|---|
| Rowers | 6 |
| SkiErgs | 6 |
| Bikes | 6 |
| Runners | 6 |
| Sled lanes | 2 |

Plus boxes, dumbbells, kettlebells, wall balls, slam balls and the rig uprights.

**Groups of six is the standing unit.** We originally planned for eight of each machine; eight
rowers will not fit that section and eight bikes and skis are not gettable. Six is the
one-size-fits-all number, it lets classes run up to 18 in the room if we ever need to, and it means
the room never has to be rearranged between sessions. Programming is built around it.

Boxes stack three a side against each wall when they are not in the workout. One runner lives on
the main gym floor because it would not fit in the lift — it carries a **do not use** sign unless a
session explicitly calls for it.

**Team maths for a full room of 20:**

- 10 pairs → 10 machines in play. Simplest to run.
- 6 trios (18) → exactly one machine per team per bank. The default.
- 5 quads (20) → 5 machines per bank with one spare for a swap-out.
- 4 teams of 5 → deep benches, best for chase and capture formats.

## 5. Gym standards

These are non-negotiable and they apply to every coach in every class.

**Before the class**
- On the floor **15 minutes** before the start. Not in the car park, on the floor.
- Room is set: machines in position, monitors on, screen showing the session board, gear out.
- You have read the session. You know what each part is for and what the scaling options are.
- Check the booking list. Know if anyone is new, and know their name before they arrive.

**Starting**
- Start on time. A class that starts at 5:02 finishes at 5:02 and that is a member's whole morning.
- Brief: what the session is, what the theme is, what "good" looks like today, where the lanes are.
  Two minutes, not six.
- Name the lanes out loud: **Base, Build, Push.** Tell people it is normal to sit in different
  lanes on different days.

**During**
- You are watching, not working out. You do not train in your own class.
- Correct movement before you correct intensity.
- Move around the room. Standing at the front for 40 minutes is not coaching.
- Use people's names. Every class, every person, at least once.
- **Lane changes are a one-to-one conversation.** You never announce that someone should drop a
  lane in front of the room.
- Anything that looks like pain rather than effort — stop it and ask.

**Finishing**
- Finish on time. Cool-down is part of the session, not an optional extra.
- Scoreboard or a closing word. People need to know how the session went.
- Pack down with the class. Coach leaves the room last.

**After**
- Log your hours.
- Send the post-class note if anything happened worth knowing (see §7).
- Anything that should change in the programming goes to Chris, not into next week's class.

**Presentation**
- TAC shirt. Clean shoes. Phone out of your hand unless you are running the timer.
- You are the first thing a member sees at 4:55am. Look like you want to be there.

## 6. Member experience standards

**Booking.** Every class is capped and members must book through the TAC App. Entering the gym
auto-marks attendance, so no separate check-in is needed provided they booked. If plans change they
should cancel promptly so someone else can take the spot. Booking is not bureaucracy — it protects
the rest of the community from missing out.

**New members.** Introduce yourself, get their name, find out if anything is injured or sore, put
them in Base for their first session regardless of what they tell you they can do, and check on
them at least three times. A new member's first class decides whether there is a second one.

**Scaling.** Everything is scalable and having scalable options is a stated focus of the club, not a
fallback. Three lanes on every target:

- **Base** — finishing in good shape matters more than the number. Halve holds, drop distances, ski
  instead of run.
- **Build** — the prescribed numbers. Most of the room sits here.
- **Push** — the top end. Only for people already holding pace with a sentence left in them.

You have watched these people train more than the board has. If someone should sit a lane higher or
lower, tell them, one to one.

**Screens and boards.** Every session has a board output that goes on the TV. The front desk team
puts it up. To get the images on the TV: output to the TAC iPhone and AirPlay to the screen. If the
connection drops, reconnect from the TAC iPhone.

**TrainHeroic.** All Strength sessions are published to TrainHeroic a week in advance. It stores
maxes, prescribes loads off them, and carries a video for every exercise. Members get a free trial
then **USD$20 per month** ongoing (current members' trial ran to 26 September). Members who miss a
class can complete the session solo from the app — say this to anyone who apologises for missing a
week. Access codes and setup instructions come from Johno / the front desk.

## 7. Safety, incidents and injuries

- Know where the first aid kit and the defib are before your first class. Ask on day one.
- Anyone reporting chest pain, dizziness or a head knock stops immediately. No negotiation.
- Any injury, near miss or member complaint: tell Chris and Johno **the same day, in writing**. A
  text is fine to start, followed by an email. Write down what happened, when, who was there and
  what you did.
- Do not diagnose. Chris is an exercise physiologist and can take the clinical side; your job is to
  stop the session and escalate.
- If a member tells you about an injury, that information goes to Chris so it is carried into their
  programming, not held in your head for the one class you coach.

## 8. Programming boundaries

**What a coach may change on the floor:** load, range of motion, distance, a machine substitution, a
movement substitution for an injury, the lane a person is in.

**What a coach may not change:** the structure of the session, the time domain, the intent of the
day (do not turn the aerobic day into intervals because the room looks bored), the order of the
parts.

**How to get something changed properly:** tell Chris after the class, with what you saw and what
you would do instead. This is how the pull-up programming changed in week one — enough people could
not do weighted pull-ups as prescribed, so the strength progression was rebuilt around that rather
than left for each coach to patch over. That loop only works if you use it.

## 10. New coach first morning — run sheet

Allow 90 minutes, before the first class or straight after it.

1. **Tour (10 min).** Group Fitness Room, strength floor, functional training zone, first aid and
   defib, staff room, the runner on the main floor that is out of service.
2. **This document (20 min).** Walk §3 to §6 together. Do not hand it over to read later.
3. **The room layout (10 min).** Groups of six, why, and the fact that gear does not move.
4. **TrainHeroic (15 min).** Set them up on the app, walk one published session, show maxes and the
   exercise videos.
5. **GymMaster and bookings (10 min).** Booking list, caps, attendance.
6. **Scaling language (10 min).** Base, Build, Push. Role-play two lane conversations, including
   telling someone to go down a lane.
7. **Screens (5 min).** Board output, TAC iPhone, AirPlay.
8. **Shadow (1 class).** They watch, they do not coach.
9. **Co-coach (1 class).** They take the brief and one part of the session, you take the rest.
10. **Solo with a check-in.** They run it, you are in the building, you debrief straight after.

**Sign-off.** A coach is cleared for a class type once they have shadowed one, co-coached one, run
one solo with a debrief, and Chris has signed it off. Record it in the TAC Ops tool.
