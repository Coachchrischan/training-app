# Roundtable review, 2026-08-16

## What ran, and what did not

15 expert reviewers across 5 disciplines (product/UX, S&C and rehab coaching, engineering,
accessibility, compliance and go-to-market), 3 distinct personas each. They produced **127
raw findings, consolidated to 50 distinct items**.

**The adversarial verification pass and the panel vote did not run.** Both hit the usage
session limit. So the severities below are the reviewers' own, not verified or vote-ranked,
and the normal "must-fix" bucket does not exist. Treat this as a strong lead list rather

I verified the top items myself by reading the code, and fixed the ones that were real.
Every "FIXED" line below was re-tested against the running API, not just edited.

## Fixed and verified tonight

| # | Finding | Why it mattered | Verification |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Deleting a session destroyed the athlete's logged results.** `results.workout_id` is `ON DELETE CASCADE`, and the only guard was a confirm() that never mentioned logged training. | Editing your own calendar could silently erase an athlete's history. | DELETE now returns 409 with the count; the coach gets a second, explicit confirm before `?force=1`. Tested: blocked with "1 logged result would be deleted with this session". |
| 2 | **Un-ticked sets counted as performed work.** The logging screen prefills every set from the prescription; the server then fed all of them into auto-maxes, tonnage, e1RM and leaderboards without checking `completed`. | Phantom PRs and inflated tonnage from a session an athlete opened and abandoned. This is the one that would have quietly corrupted your coaching data. | Added a shared `completedSets()` gate on every server path. Tested: logging 2 un-ticked sets at 200 kg added 0 kg tonnage and created no max. |
| 3 | **Team calendars leaked teammates' private data.** Results and task completions were fetched by workout id with no athlete filter, so any team member received others' session comments, weights and readiness scores. | A straight privacy breach, and the exact class of thing that matters under the Privacy Act once real clients are on it. | Athletes now only ever receive their own rows; the owning coach still sees everyone. Tested: Jono's calendar returns 0 foreign rows. |
| 4 | **Messaging had no authorisation at all.** Any signed-in user could read any team thread and send a message to any user id. | Cross-client messaging in a product whose whole premise is a private coach relationship. | Explicit relationship checks both directions (coach to own athletes/teams, athlete to own coach/teams), 404 otherwise. Tested: Jono to Abbey blocked 404, foreign team blocked 404, own team still readable. |
| 5 | **Exiting a part-logged session discarded everything**, with no confirmation, from a small ghost button. | Worst for BlueZone clients: one mis-tap loses a whole session of keyed numbers. | Exit now offers "Save my progress and leave" (writes a partial result), "Keep training", or an explicit discard. |
| 6 | **Builder autosave races.** The debounced PUT was cancelled on unmount, so closing dropped the last edit, and the completion handler could mark state "saved" that had since changed. Publish flipped its label before anything persisted. | Silent loss of programming work. | Close now flushes a pending save and warns if it fails; an edit counter means only the state actually sent is marked saved; failures surface as visible red text instead of nothing. |
| 7 | **Epley e1RM had no rep cap.** A set of 20 could redefine an athlete's working max. | Rubbish maxes driving every %1RM prescription downstream. | Capped at 10 reps in one shared `e1rm()` used by both the auto-max engine and analytics. |
| 8 | **BlueZone could not meet its own accessibility floors.** Body was a fixed 16px with no brand override; targets were 44px and 34px against an agreed 48px floor; the type sizing in px ignored the browser's own text-size setting. | The Diabetes app brief set these floors for exactly this audience, and the brand system had no way to express them. | Added `--font-size-base`, `--control-min` and `--control-min-sm` to every brand pack. BlueZone now renders 18px body and 48px controls with nothing shrinking. Tested in the browser: body 18px, one remaining sub-48 control found and fixed. |
| 9 | **Active nav state was colour-alone**, and BlueZone's primary (#004e93) and muted (#46557f) are near-identical dark blues, roughly 1.3:1 against each other. | The current-tab indicator effectively did not exist in that brand. | Active tabs now carry a 4px top bar and bold weight as well as colour. |
| 10 | **Calendar actions were hover-only** (`hidden group-hover:flex`), including the only way to remove a health task. | Unreachable by keyboard and on a tablet. | Always visible, with proper aria-labels. |

Also added a global `:focus-visible` ring, and replaced the athlete Messages screen's
coach-id guesswork with a proper `coachId` on `/auth/me`.

## Top open items, not yet actioned

Ranked by my read, since the panel vote did not complete:

1. **No week-level or cross-athlete reuse.** Everything is one session at a time: no "copy
   this week", no apply-template-to-athlete, no multi-select. Three separate reviewers
   raised it independently. This is the single biggest thing standing between v1 and it
   being genuinely faster than TrainHeroic for you.
2. **In-progress logging is still only saved at Exit or Finish.** The exit guard covers the
   mis-tap case, but a phone lock or tab discard mid-session still loses the log. Proper fix
   is a debounced partial save on every set tick.
3. **Modals have no focus trap or dialog semantics**, and the full-screen builder overlay
   leaves the sidebar focusable underneath. Native `<dialog>` would fix both.
4. **Jargon is unexplained for non-lifters**: RPE, RIR, e1RM, "working max", "tonnage"
   appear with no first-use explanation. Material for BlueZone, and honestly for a lot of
   1:1 clients too.
5. **No rate limiting or lockout on login**, and invite codes are 8 hex characters that
   never expire. Fine locally, must harden before it is on the internet.
6. **AU health-data compliance**: once real client weights, readiness and pain comments are
   in it, the Privacy Act and NDB obligations bite. The BlueZone `BACKEND-OPTIONS.md` brief
   already has the answer (Supabase, Sydney, RLS); it needs actioning at hosting time, plus
   a privacy policy and a consent model.
7. **Exercise library licensing.** The seed is derived from TrainHeroic's library. Fine for
   your own use; a real risk if this is ever sold or given to another organisation. Worth a
   clean-source library before any commercial move.
