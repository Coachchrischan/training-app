# TAC Coach Reporting

**What coaches report, to whom, how often, and what happens to it.**

Version 1.0 · 16 September 2026 · Owner: Chris Chan (Coach & Programming Manager)

This is the answer to the question *"what should actually be in the reporting document, for new
coaches and for current coaches?"* It is deliberately opinionated and deliberately small. A
reporting system that asks for more than a coach will actually fill in at 6:05am is a reporting
system that produces nothing.

---

## The principle

**Every report must change something.** If a field on a form has never once caused a programming
change, a roster change or a conversation, delete the field.

There are three loops, and they run at different speeds:

| Loop | Speed | Who | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| Post-class note | Per class, 60 seconds | Coach → Chris | Fix the programming before it is delivered again |
| Monthly coach meeting | Monthly, 1 hour, paid | All coaches | Align standards, surface patterns, make decisions |
| Coach review | 4 weeks (new), quarterly (current) | Chris → coach | Develop the coach |

---

## 1. The post-class note

**60 seconds, on the phone, before leaving the building.** Four fields. Nothing else.

1. **Attendance** — booked vs actually in the room.
2. **What did not work** — the movement, the time domain or the explanation that fell over. Blank
   is a legitimate answer and most days it will be blank.
3. **Who needs a note** — anyone new, anyone sore, anyone who looked off. Names, one line each.
4. **Lane drift** — was the room mostly in Base, Build or Push? If everyone sat in Base, the
   prescription was wrong, not the room.

That is it. No RPE averages, no session summaries, no "how did you feel it went". Those produce
prose nobody reads.

**Where it goes.** Into the TAC Ops tool as a class note against the date. It lands in Chris's
week, alongside the KPI board and the reflections log.

**What happens to it.** Chris reads all of them once a week when writing the next block. Field 2
and field 4 are the inputs to programming changes. Field 3 is the input to member follow-up.

**Worked example — the one that already happened.** Week one of the new strength block prescribed
weighted pull-ups. Enough of the room could not do a strict bodyweight pull-up that the prescription
was meaningless for them. That came back through the floor, and the progression was rebuilt around
a proper pull-up ladder with a real entry point. That is the whole system working: a coach saw it,
said it, the program changed, and no coach had to quietly invent their own patch. Without field 2,
that is three coaches each improvising something different for a month.

## 2. The monthly coach meeting

Johno's rule: **one meeting a month, capped at an hour, with a clear agenda, paid at the standard
rate.** No agenda published 48 hours out means the meeting does not run.

**Standing agenda (60 min):**

| Time | Item |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:05 | Numbers on the board: attendance by class, cancellation rate, new members through the door |
| 0:05–0:15 | What came back from the floor this month: the post-class notes, grouped into themes |
| 0:15–0:30 | The next block: what is changing, why, and what coaches need to say to members about it |
| 0:30–0:45 | One standard we are tightening this month. One. Not five |
| 0:45–0:55 | Open floor: anything a coach needs from Chris, Johno or Alanah |
| 0:55–1:00 | Decisions and owners, read back out loud |

**Output:** a decisions list with a name against each one, in the TAC Ops tool. Not minutes. If an
item has no owner it did not get decided.

## 3. New coach: the first four weeks

A new coach is not signed off because they have been here a month. They are signed off against a
list.

**Clearance per class type** (from the handbook): shadow one → co-coach one → run one solo with a
debrief → Chris signs off. Recorded per class type, not once globally. Being cleared for Conditioning
does not clear you for Strength.

**Week 1 — observed, debriefed same day.**
- Did the class start on time?
- Was the brief under three minutes and did it include the theme and the lanes?
- Did they use names?
- Did they move around the room?
- Did they correct movement before intensity?

**Week 2 — the scaling check.** The single hardest skill and the one that separates a coach from a
timer operator. Watch specifically for: did they put a new member in Base regardless of what the
member claimed, did they hold a lane conversation one to one rather than in front of the room, and
did they catch the person who was above their lane before that person blew up?

**Week 3 — the room check.** Set-up, pack-down, screens, bookings, gear back where it lives. This is
the stuff that makes the next coach's class possible.

**Week 4 — the member check.** Not an opinion, a number: how many members in that coach's classes
came back the following week. Plus two or three direct member comments, gathered casually.

**End of week 4 written review — one page, four headings:** what they are already good at, the one
thing to fix next, what they are cleared to coach solo, and what they are not cleared for yet and
what it will take. Kept in the tool. It is the document you point back to in three months.

## 4. Current coach: the quarterly review

Same page, shorter conversation, different weighting. A current coach is not being assessed on
whether they can run a class. They are being developed.

**The four questions:**

1. **Retention.** Are the members in your classes still here? Class-level retention over the quarter,
   not gym-wide.
2. **Delivery.** Start time, finish time, brief quality, room state. Sampled, not surveilled — Chris
   drops into a class unannounced once a quarter.
3. **Contribution.** What came back from you through the post-class notes that changed the
   programming? A coach who never sends field 2 is either coaching a perfect program or not
   watching.
4. **Development.** What is the one thing you want to be better at by the next review, and what does
   the club owe you to get there — a course, a shadow session, a block of programming to write?

**What it is not.** It is not a performance management process and it is not a surprise. Anything
serious enough for a formal conversation should have been said on the day it happened, not banked
for a quarterly.

## 5. What we actually measure

Coach-level, monthly, on one board:

| Metric | Why it is here | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Class attendance vs cap | The product's health | 70%+ of cap |
| Booked-but-absent rate | Whether booking discipline is holding | Under 15% |
| Week-on-week member return in your classes | The only retention number that is yours | 80%+ |
| Classes started on time | The standard that is easiest to hold and most visible to members | 100% |
| Post-class notes submitted | Whether the feedback loop exists at all | 100% of classes |
| New members given a Base lane and three check-ins | First-class experience | 100% |

Club-level, monthly:

- Total attendance by class type and time slot, so we know which of the new 7am slots are actually
  working.
- TrainHeroic active users and conversion off the trial.
- Community Day attendance and repeat rate, which is the number that tells us whether the community
  product is working.
- Programming changes made, and where each one came from. If none came from the floor in a month,
  the post-class note has become a formality.

## 6. Templates

**Post-class note (in the tool, and as a text you can send):**

```
Class:        [Conditioning Mon 5:30am]
Coach:        [name]
Booked / in:  [14 / 11]
Did not work: [the renegade rows at 3-min intervals - transitions were a mess with 16 in the room]
Notes on:     [Sarah - new, Base lane, came back for the cool-down question, follow up]
              [Tom - right shoulder, subbed ski for row]
Lane drift:   [mostly Base. Build was too far away today]
```

**Monthly meeting agenda (published 48 hours out):**

```
TAC Coach Meeting - [date]
Numbers:      [attendance, cancellations, new members]
From the floor: [3 themes, max]
Next block:   [what changes and what to tell members]
Standard:     [the one we are tightening]
Open floor
Decisions + owners
```

**Coach review (one page, four headings):**

```
Already strong:
The one thing to fix:
Cleared to coach solo:
Not cleared yet, and what it takes:
```

## 7. What deliberately is not in here

- **Session RPE averages per class.** Interesting in a one-to-one programming context, noise at
  class level.
- **Long written class reflections.** Chris keeps a reflections log because he is the programmer and
  the patterns matter to him. Asking eight coaches for prose produces eight documents nobody reads.
- **Member satisfaction surveys.** Retention is the survey. If people come back, the class works.
- **Peer coach scoring.** It poisons a small team faster than anything else.
